# S25 — Acoustic Sculpture

Implemented original seven-plate copper instrument and authored Lithuanian
content for Vėjo archyvas, explicitly fictional. A is a narrow spatial narrative
beside a broad sticky sculpture study; B is a three-part sound workbench with
actual two-slot configuration comparison. Both generate a validated local enquiry
and copy exactly that text, including selected scenario and character.

## Actual visual review

Viewed all four final PNGs: desktop A 1440x3763 (312235-byte initial measurement),
B 1440x2846, mobile A 390x4962 and B 390x3969; all well below 1 MiB. Final byte
counts and source/output hashes are authoritative in previews-manifest.json.
The copper's lighting direction was corrected after viewing the initial dark
front faces. The final warm faces, seven thin extrusions and dark grounded base
are clearly visible. Reduced motion fixes the final split state independently of
initial IntersectionObserver timing; ordinary scrolling still animates the split.

A: large three-line humanist invitation, seven-part study and slender numbered
spatial stories. Intentional right-side breathing space accommodates the sticky
object while the reader moves through stories. Mobile moves the complete object
above the introduction and keeps buttons/fields at ordinary reading size.
B: broader introduction, left scenario/right character controls around the study,
real comparison tray and horizontal process ledger. Mobile groups remain separate
and useful instead of shrinking the desktop three-region workbench.

Compared actual ready Soft Spatial and Y2K Chrome desktop previews. Against Soft
Spatial: thin matte metal segmentation instead of one soft clay volume; an
explicit character/comparison workbench instead of layered service explanation;
and scenario-to-enquiry navigation instead of passive form observations. Against
Y2K Chrome: restrained humanist/serif hierarchy instead of heavy pop poster;
matte vertical copper instead of chrome sphere/orbit; practical spatial choices
and real sound instead of an edition index. These include structural differences.
