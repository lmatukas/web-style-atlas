Keep these features of the direction: original cutout-like objects with pale edges; deliberate overlap inside reserved illustration stages; warm playful colour relationships; friendly large headings with plain practical text.

Composition A: an assembled cutout scene inviting visitors to a making day. Composition B: an illustrated three-chapter story with sticker notes. The brief below describes both; build the chosen one. B has a different content structure and flow of use, not just a different order. In both versions keep the same palette, type voice and honesty rules.

## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Say once, in the footer, that the brand is invented, and once beside any form or download that nothing is sent. Headings name the tables, the paper and the day, keeping the playful tone without slogans; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is SNIPWHISTLE, an invented community making day with paper, pencils and scissors. The tone is lively and welcoming, but the interface stays predictable. The event, its day and its activities are fictional; say so in a practical note. There is no booking, ticket or registration.

Palette: warm paper #fff2d7, a mustard surface #f2d994, deep purple text #3b2255, muted text #65526b, a purple accent #5b338c with cream text #fff6e5, borders #836c8f, a rust focus outline #8d342c, and a teal field #246b61 with cream text #fff7e3. Use one friendly sans serif (Arial or a licensed rounded sans) with large, warm headings and plain body text.

Draw three original vector cutouts with a consistent pale cut edge and simple shading: a yellow pencil with a coral eraser, coral-handled craft scissors and a teal folded paper plane. They are finished illustrations, not placeholders or traced clip art. Give each a descriptive alt text, or hide it when a caption already says the same.

Composition A, the invitation: a compact identity with links (what happens, good to know, the story). A two-part opening: large friendly words such as "Fold, draw, cut for a whole Saturday." with one sentence and a link down the page, beside a reserved scene where the pencil, scissors and plane overlap deliberately. A short event strip with fictional context and a practical-note link. Three numbered activity rows (fold something, make a mark, change the shape), each with a heading and two plain sentences. A dark purple closing band links to the story. A native "What is this making day?" disclosure and a colophon finish the page.

Composition B, the story: a story heading and three chapter links. Chapter 1 pairs the folded plane with a short narrative across the width. Chapter 2 reverses the image and text and adds a small sticker-like note outside the body measure. Chapter 3 puts a generous coloured note field beside the pencil. Close with a practical disclosure about the event and illustrations, and a return link.

Overlap only happens inside illustration stages; text and controls keep their own clear space, and focus outlines are never clipped. No bouncing tools, spinning stickers or scroll-driven paper flight. On a phone the scene shrinks as one unit above the words, stickers move into the flow and every chapter reads image then text.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: the composition is complete when still; short link transitions disappear with prefers-reduced-motion. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Activity, notes and story links reach real sections; the chapter index works; practical disclosures open with a keyboard; no illustration covers text or a focused control, and none is a disguised button.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
