## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Say once, in the footer, that the brand is invented, and once beside any form or download that nothing is sent. Keep the safety notes next to the drawings, but do not repeat "fictional" in every paragraph; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is FORM / SYSTEMS, an invented maker of concept enclosures. The subject is the fictional M–04 transfer module, a rectangular enclosure with a round inlet collar, compared with a larger M–06. The numbers are authored concept values, not a product: state clearly that nothing is certified, manufactured, available or suitable for installation, and give no performance, load or safety claims.

Palette: pale grey-green sheet #e9ece8, a near-white surface #f8f9f4, dark slate text #202a2c, grey labels #4c595b, a dark brown accent #5a3e08, rules #6b7777, a blue focus outline #1643a0 and one yellow signal colour #e2c35a used only for a bounded document strip. Use one plain sans serif (Arial or a licensed technical sans) with tabular numbers, and a monospace face only if needed for drawing labels.

Draw two original inline SVG elevations that are geometrically honest. Front: a 300 × 180 mm envelope with a centred Ø 120 mm inlet and four corner fixing marks (for example 300 mm → 480 units, so 180 → 288 and Ø 120 → 192). Side: 120 mm depth at the same height with the projecting collar. Add dimension lines and labels, and caption each "Illustrative, not for manufacture"; state that screen scale is not production scale.

Composition A, the component sheet: a compact document header with links (product sheet, compare assemblies, document notes). A three-part product plate: the identifier "M–04 Transfer module." with a one-line descriptor and "Concept component / Revision A", the front elevation, and a narrow status strip. Then a dimensional record as a definition list (width 300 mm, height 180 mm, depth 120 mm, inlet Ø 120 mm, drawing status). A side projection whose two callouts link depth and inlet back to those rows. A numbered assembly sequence — housing, inlet collar, corner fixings — each with a joint note, followed by the sentence that the order explains the drawing and is not an assembly procedure. Close with document notes and a native disclosure of the drawing boundary, and a link to the comparison.

Composition B, the comparison: open with the question ("M–04 and M–06 side by side.") instead of the product title. A small drawing-set index beside matched front and side elevations that share their height. A comparison ledger as an HTML table with row headers: width 300 / 420 mm, height 180 / 240 mm, depth 120 / 160 mm, inlet Ø 120 / Ø 160 mm, status "Illustrative concept" for both. Then a "Before choosing" note (a larger envelope is not a better product), a limitations disclosure and a return link.

Every apparent action has a real destination: no empty CAD download, quote form or product selector. Never use random numbers or inconsistent dimension lines as decoration. On a phone the product plate stacks identifier, drawing, status; drawings scale inside their frame; the table keeps short column headings and readable rows without tiny text.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: content is present immediately; nonessential transitions and smooth scrolling disappear with prefers-reduced-motion. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Drawing geometry matches the stated dimensions; spec values are selectable text with units; the comparison is a real HTML table; document-note disclosures work with a keyboard; no download, quote or buy action exists without a real destination.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
