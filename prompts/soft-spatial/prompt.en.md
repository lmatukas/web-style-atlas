## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Say once, in the footer, that the brand is invented, and once beside any form or download that nothing is sent. Headings name the lamp, its materials and the three layers; softness is a visual quality, not a word to repeat; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is HALVESSA, an invented spatial practice that presents one ambient-object study, the Pebble, and explains its work in three connected layers: atmosphere, arrangement and detail. The Pebble is an illustration, not a product: give no light output, material or price. Say that the practice and its studies are fictional, and that no enquiry is submitted.

Palette: chalk #f4f4ed, a pale sage surface #e6eae2, dark green text #263b35, muted text #54675c, a deep green accent #305b4d with white text, borders #7b8b7e and a plum focus outline #6d3a62; in the artwork, add a pale blush within the same family. Use one calm sans serif (Arial or a licensed humanist sans). Text stays dark and flat; never emboss labels or lower contrast to look soft.

Depth comes from consistent light, not a rendering engine. Draw everything in CSS: the Pebble is an elliptical shell with a shallow recessed opening, lit from a window on the left, standing on a floor line with a long soft contact shadow falling to the right. All other volumes — discs, platforms, rounded blocks, a recessed rounded surface — use the same light direction and their own contact shadows.

Composition A, the object: a visible practice name and compact navigation (the object, our layers, notes). A short invitation ("A pebble-shaped lamp by the window.") with one line and a link, left of a stage where the Pebble stands in a small room with the window glow, floor line and shadow. The caption names the window as the light source. Three short notes describe what the object does in that light (the window does the lighting, the shadow sets it on the floor, nothing moves to be seen). Two enlarged material samples (chalk shell, sage interior) with a note that they are illustrated materials. A native form-note disclosure and a link to the layers page close it.

Composition B, the practice: a broad typographic introduction ("Atmosphere, layout and detail.") and an anchored link down the page. Three layers step down the page as an indented staircase. Atmosphere is primary — the largest vignette and strongest heading, because its light governs the others; Arrangement uses raised volumes; Detail uses a recessed rounded surface. Each layer ends with a definition list (focus, study output). A closing line ties the layers together and links back to the Pebble; practice notes with a scope disclosure finish the page.

Do not replace layers with repeated rounded dashboard cards, or add perpetual floating, spinning or scroll-driven camera travel. Pill links have real destinations and clear focus separated from shadows. On a phone the stage keeps its proportions above the text, and the staircase becomes a gentle single column that still steps in size.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: no floating, spinning or camera travel; prefers-reduced-motion removes short transitions. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Every object shares one light direction and has a contact shadow; pill links reach real sections or pages; the scope disclosures work with a keyboard; text stays flat and readable; nothing can be booked or bought.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
