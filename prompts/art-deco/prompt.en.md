## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Mark the working demonstration name clearly as invented; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is THE ATRIUM, an invented small hotel built around a winter garden, which hosts an imagined evening of piano and conversation called "The Velvet Hour". The venue, evening, programme and rooms are fictional; say so beside the invitation and in the guest notes. The style borrows a geometric vocabulary, not a historical building: never claim historic authenticity.

Palette: deep green #102b2c, a slightly lighter green surface #193a39, warm cream text #f7efd9, muted sage text #bfcbbe, a pale gold accent #e2c888 with dark green text, sage rules #819781 and a white focus outline. Use Georgia (or a licensed geometric display serif) for titles and Arial for practical text, with spaced uppercase labels. Gold is for rules, ornaments and one action, not body text.

Draw ornaments in CSS or SVG and make each one structural: a fan crowning the invitation, a diamond marking a pause, double fine rules framing the invitation, and a stepped tower elevation with repeated windows for the house. Decorative ornaments are hidden from assistive technology; the house elevation gets a short descriptive label.

Composition A, the invitation: a balanced header with a spaced name (A T R I U M) and links (the evening, the house, guest notes). A centred invitation between two architectural rules: the fan crown, whose three segments are links to the timed movements (18:30 doors, 19:00 piano, 20:15 after); "The Atrium presents"; a large uppercase title; one line; a diamond; the date and time; a "Discover the evening" anchor link; and "Demonstration programme · No tickets are sold". Then the symmetry breaks: a numbered introduction beside three timed movements, each with a time, a short title and one sentence. A house note with its heading on the left and paragraph on the right links to the house story. A native disclosure with the programme and venue note closes the page.

Composition B, the house story: a two-column arrival — an expressive serif statement ("Some places invite you to stay.") and one line beside the stepped elevation, with a link to the rooms. A broad idea section pairs a central statement with two readable paragraphs. Two room records with Roman numerals (the Garden Room, the Lantern Suite), each with one sentence, a fact list (floor, outlook, layout, status: illustrative concept only) and a native disclosure with the full concept. Guest notes link back to the evening.

Do not repeat the same double-rule frame in every section or let any ornament link nowhere. On a phone the invitation stays centred but narrower, the fan scales as one unit with tappable segments, and room facts stack as label then value.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: content is present immediately; prefers-reduced-motion removes short transitions and smooth scrolling. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: The fan links to the three timed movements and every ornament leads to content; house and room links reach real sections; room-concept disclosures work with a keyboard; no ticket, booking, price or availability is implied.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
