## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Say once, in the footer, that the brand is invented, and once beside any form or download that nothing is sent. Write concrete observations with times and places on the walk, not reflections about slowing down; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is CAIRNWISE, an invented notebook of small journeys. It records what was noticed and when, not a destination advertisement. All notes are fictional; the sketch illustrates a feeling and is never presented as evidence of a real place or route. Do not invent coordinates, distances or bookings.

Palette: warm cream #f4efdf, a pale sand note panel #e7ddc5, dark brown text #3b3426, secondary labels #665c48, a muted rust accent #875039 for cross-references, rules #8b7d63 and a brown focus outline #70482e. Use Georgia for the journal title and narrative headings, Arial for body text and the system monospace stack for short field labels such as notebook numbers and times. Do not simulate handwriting for navigation, credits or practical details. Texture, if any, stays faint and never sits behind text.

Draw two original assets: a pencil-style SVG sketch of layered tree crowns with pale mist lines and a dashed circle marking an opening, and a schematic SVG loop with three numbered stops (the gate, the clearing, return). Caption the loop "not to scale, not a real location".

Composition A, the fragment: a small notebook identity with links to the fragment, the route sketch and the diary. A label such as "Notebook 02 / Fragment 07", a short title and one line of context. The main column holds the framed sketch with its caption and a short narrative observation. A narrower margin holds three numbered notes (first impression, a small detail, keep in mind) and a native disclosure about the drawing source. Later, the route sketch pairs a three-item list with the loop diagram and its caption, and a closing note links to the diary.

Composition B, the route diary: a compact diary title and a one-line summary, a local index of the three stops, and three chronological entries, each with a monospace time and stop label, a heading and a short observation. The middle entry holds the sketch with a caption explaining its illustrative role. A preparation rail holds the route diagram and three real native checkboxes (a notebook, a pencil, a question worth keeping) under the line "Local checklist. Changes are not saved." Close with a sources disclosure and a link back to the fragment.

The relationship between narrative and margin (A) or time and entry (B) is the design; do not turn the notebook into a grid of cards or hide notes behind hover. On a phone, annotations move below the introduction they belong to, diary times stack above their entries, every sketch keeps its caption and the route diagram scales inside its frame without sideways scrolling.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: confirm that sketches, captions and notes stay still, and that optional link transitions disappear with prefers-reduced-motion. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Fragment, route and diary links reach real sections; the source disclosures work; the three preparation checkboxes change state with mouse, touch and keyboard and say plainly that nothing is saved.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
