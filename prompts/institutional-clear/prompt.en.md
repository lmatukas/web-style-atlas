## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Mark the working demonstration name clearly as invented; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is QUILLMERE COMMUNITY CENTRE, an invented public building where four services share one ground floor. A visitor arrives with one task and needs to know where to go. A banner at the top states that the building and services are fictional and that no applications are accepted. Never invent official rules, legal requirements, phone numbers or application buttons.

Palette: white #ffffff, a pale blue-grey surface #edf2f5, dark blue-grey text #20303d, secondary text #50616e, a deep blue accent #164d70 with white text, one yellow signal colour #f2c94c for letter tiles and the you-are-here marker, borders #768895 and a strong focus outline #0b527f. Use one clear sans serif (Arial or a licensed public-signage sans) with large, sign-like headings. The header is white on a heavy dark rule, not a coloured band with a centred search box.

Draw original inline SVG plans with rectangles, paths and short labels, coloured only with your tokens. Each plan has a title and description, and the same information appears as nearby text. Furniture is drawn lightly so the letters and route dominate.

Composition A, the building directory: an arrival statement ("Find the right room, desk or service.") with a short explanation and a framed "You are here: main entrance, ground floor" note, beside a ground-floor plan with areas A–D lettered on the plan. Under a heavy rail, the directory heading shares a row with a compact search (label, input, Clear button, a help line and a polite count such as "4 services shown"). Four lettered columns follow: letter tile, location line ("Area A · first door on the left"), task heading and one sentence. Search filters these columns, ignores accents and case, and shows "No matching service. Try a shorter word or clear the search." when empty. Then lettered native disclosures explaining what to bring to areas B, C and D, and a three-stop dotted route showing how a room request moves, linking to the Area A guide.

Composition B, the Area A guide: a breadcrumb back to the directory, a large area sign with letter A and its location, a title ("Use a community room"), a summary and a status label ("Example guide · fictional rules · no booking on this site"), beside a zoomed plan of Area A with rooms A1 and A2. Under a heavy rail, a numbered route rail on the left links to four sections: before you begin (a checklist), room options (A1 meeting room, A2 activity room — the same codes as the plan), how a request is reviewed (a horizontal dotted route of three stops) and questions and help (native disclosures, including "Can I book through this website? No."). End with a return link.

Navigation stays visible and wraps on small screens; no icon-only controls, hover menus or drawers. On a phone the plan follows the arrival statement, directory columns stack with their letter tiles, the route rail becomes an inline list and dotted routes turn vertical.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: the page is still; search updates without moving focus or animating rows; optional transitions disappear with prefers-reduced-motion. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Search filters the four services by title and description, announces the count politely, shows an empty state and clears with a real button; plan letters, directory tiles and notes use the same codes; disclosures and route links work with a keyboard; no application or booking is implied.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
