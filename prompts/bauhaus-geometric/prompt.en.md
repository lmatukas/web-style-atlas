## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Mark the working demonstration name clearly as invented; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is GRIDMOOT, an invented two-day gathering about form, information and everyday use, with talks, a workshop and an open studio. It is a contemporary interpretation of elementary geometry: never present it as a historical institution, a reproduction of a historic poster or an affiliated event. Every date, venue and session is fictional.

Palette: warm paper #f1eadb, a sand surface #e0d6c3, near-black text #202020, muted labels #5f584f, signal red #b93122, deep blue #184a88 (text on it #f8f1e5), yellow #e4b52d, rules #746c60 and a blue focus outline #134d80. Use one plain sans serif (Arial or a licensed geometric sans) in large sizes aligned to the grid modules. Draw every shape in CSS or inline SVG; no image files are needed.

Composition A, the event poster: a small header with the event name, "contemporary form / fictional event" and links to the programme, the idea and the exhibition. A poster grid: the headline (for example "form meets function.") fills one large module and a geometric field of a red disc, a blue square and a yellow triangle fills the other; a compact date strip ties them together, with a link to the programme. The programme follows as three short timed rows (time / format, title, one-line description), not enclosed cards. A wide statement pairs a blue plane with a short explanation of the premise. Close with a native disclosure of practical details that states nothing is sold or collected, and a link to the exhibition.

Composition B, the exhibition: a compact introduction and a local index of three works (balance, direction, sequence). Present three original composition studies on unequal modules: a red disc meeting a blue square, a triangular diagonal crossing a square field with a small return bar, and a wide sequence of circles changing scale with one deliberate interruption. Attach each work's number, title and two-sentence explanation of its composition. A closing process note says these are contemporary studies and links back to the event.

Shapes must establish weight, balance or a boundary between content areas; never paste small decorative icons onto a generic hero-and-cards page, and never use thick outlined stickers or random rotations. Decorative shapes are hidden from assistive technology; captions carry their meaning. On a phone the poster becomes headline, geometry, date strip in that order, modules stack without losing their proportions, and no shape ever overlaps text.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: the geometry is complete without movement; optional link transitions disappear with prefers-reduced-motion. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Programme and exhibition links reach real sections; the practical-details disclosure works with a keyboard; no shape covers a control or text; no fake ticketing.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
