## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Mark the working demonstration name clearly as invented; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is VARROW EDITIONS, an invented seating collection: a Frame chair and a Long bench that share one idea — a soft cushion held just apart from an open timber frame. They are visual studies, not products: no price, availability, timber species, textile composition or manufacturing claim. Say so near the collection and in the study notes.

Palette: warm off-white #f7f5ef, a pale stone surface #e8e4da, dark warm grey text #302c27, muted labels #625d54, a dark walnut accent #594432 with light text #faf8f1, rules #8a8377 and a brown focus outline #6b4930. Use Georgia (or a licensed refined serif) for short titles and Arial for labels, dimensions and notes. Restraint means fewer elements and real explanation, not lower contrast.

Draw original SVG studies: the Frame chair (pale curved cushions in an open dark timber frame), the Long bench (one long pale cushion on four slender legs), an enlarged junction of the chair (stitched seam following the curve, a visible gap, timber grain below) and a paired front elevation of both pieces at one shared illustrative scale. Give inline figures a title and description.

Composition A, the object scene: a left-aligned studio header with links (the detail, editions, collection document). An unequal scene: a short serif title ("A softer edge.") and one line on the left; one large Frame chair on the right with a caption "01 / Frame chair" linking to its dimensions. A numbered note sits lower, beside the chair, explaining the small gap between cushion and frame. Then a sudden change of scale: the large enlarged junction, with its description offset to the lower right ("Softness, held in place."), saying it is an enlarged illustration with no magnification claim. One companion bench follows ("One language. Two proportions."), then a short factual note that the collection is fictional and a link to the document.

Composition B, the collection document: a document heading ("An exercise in proportion."), a line stating dimensions are illustrative centimetres and not construction drawings, then a full-width measurement sheet: the two elevations on a shared baseline with codes 01 and 02. Separate records repeat width, depth and height in readable HTML (Frame chair 74 × 78 × 76 cm; Long bench 160 × 46 × 44 cm). A two-column material register explains the shared seam, gap and visible frame, with a link back to the enlarged junction. A native disclosure on dimensions, manufacture and care, and a return link close the page.

Avoid a centred hero followed by two near-identical full-chair frames, a long decorative quotation instead of material explanation, and repeating the chair without a change of scale or task. On a phone the object comes after the title at a generous size, notes follow the image they explain, and the elevation sheet scales as one drawing with records below.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: objects and measurements stay still; prefers-reduced-motion removes short transitions and smooth scrolling. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Object codes and section links reach real destinations; elevations share one scale and match the dimensions written in HTML; the study-notes disclosure works with a keyboard; no price, purchase, availability or contact form.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
