## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Say once, in the footer, that the brand is invented, and once beside any form or download that nothing is sent. Keep the reporting voice; do not repeat "fictional" or "illustrative" in every caption and byline; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is THE COMMON LEDGER, an invented local demonstration paper. Its lead story looks at an illustrative proposal to extend the opening hours of four fictional branch libraries around one square. Every person, place and number is invented; say so in the copy and in an editorial record. Never present the content as real news, a council decision or a forecast.

Palette: newsprint #f4f0e6, a supporting surface #e7e2d6, near-black text #21211f, secondary labels #57574f, one deep red accent #8b291c for proposed values and key links, rules #757268 and a strong blue focus outline #163ba1. Use Georgia for the masthead and headlines and Arial for body, captions and labels, with system fallbacks and no remote fonts. Use tabular numbers in tables and charts.

Composition A, the front page: an issue line with volume, issue and date; a substantial masthead with a short motto; a department rule with links (City, Culture, Analysis, The record). The lead takes the full width: kicker, a two-line question headline, a standfirst and byline above a rule, then an original SVG plan of four branch boxes around a hatched square beside the lead copy (about seven to five columns). Each box states weekly hours as current → proposed, and each connector carries the change. Caption it as a schematic, not a street map. Below, an unequal brief strip: a short neighbourhood note, a wide numbers column with the total change and an original SVG bar chart of current and proposed hours (change printed under each pair), and a narrow opinion note. Then a community notebook with three short observations, an "About this edition" note and a native disclosure with the editorial record.

Composition B, the analysis: a large analytic headline, standfirst and byline, a narrow left contents rail (the question, the ledger, the method) and one main column. A full-width branch ledger interrupts the prose: an HTML table with Branch, Current, Proposed and Change columns, row headers, units, a caption and an exact total row. Method notes explain the arithmetic (change = proposed − current, total = sum of rows) and what the numbers leave out. End with a return link to the front page.

Check every calculation; the plan, chart and table must agree. Links navigate and native disclosures reveal real information; do not simulate a live ticker, subscriber login or empty search. The lead must stay visibly stronger than the briefs, and the centre brief column must hold the chart, not filler.

At about 900 px the brief strip becomes two columns with the numbers column spanning both rows; below 700 px the front page becomes lead, plan, briefs, notebook and record. On B the rail becomes an inline contents list and the table keeps readable rows with short headings. Never shrink a broadsheet into tiny text on a phone.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: content must be present immediately, and nonessential transitions and smooth scrolling must disappear with prefers-reduced-motion. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Every figure in the plan, chart and table adds up and carries its unit; the table is real selectable HTML with row and column headers; disclosures and section links work with a keyboard.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
