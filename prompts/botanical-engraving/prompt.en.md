## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Mark the working demonstration name clearly as invented; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is FOLIUM, an invented garden cabinet of three original line studies: garden sage (Salvia officinalis L.), lavender (Lavandula angustifolia Mill.) and rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus Spenn.). If you use other plants, check each accepted name against a botanical name database and link that source; do not copy its images or text. Plate numbers, the collection number and the notes are invented. State clearly that the drawings are stylised and that the page is not a guide for identifying or foraging plants. Give no care, medical or culinary advice.

Palette: pale herbarium paper #f2eee1, a sage-grey surface #e6e5d5, deep green-black text #263e32, secondary labels #57614c, a dark green accent #36523b, rules #78816d and a rust focus outline #8b341e. Use Georgia for titles and names (accepted names in italic) and Arial for body text and labels, with system fallbacks and no remote fonts.

Draw three original SVG plates with stems, leaf outlines, midribs and fine vein hatching: a broad paired-leaf sage, a lavender with narrow leaves and three flower spikes, and a rosemary with many narrow leaves on branching stems. Each needs a different silhouette, not one repeated generic leaf, and a descriptive alt text.

Composition A, plant and origin story: an understated cabinet identity with links (from the garden, the herbarium, plate records) and a small collection line. A broad three-part opening: the title, the accepted name and a short invitation to look closely; a large sage plate with a caption "original line study, not an identification plate"; and a narrow specimen label with common name, accepted name and record. Then two short origin paragraphs, two smaller companion plates beside a cabinet introduction with a link to the herbarium, and a native disclosure with name sources and illustration rights.

Composition B, the classified herbarium: an introduction and a Roman-numbered index (I, II, III) linking to three full horizontal records. Each record places the plate and its caption beside the common name, the italic accepted name, a study focus, a series line, a one-sentence observation and a native disclosure with the record source. A closing note explains the system — image, name, observation — and links back to the story.

Separate records with rules, not product cards. Keep hatching inside the drawings and the reading surface clean. No fake specimen sales or care selectors. On a phone each plate stays whole above its record, names never shrink below readable size and the index stays a simple list of links.

Functional acceptance: The three plates are distinct original drawings; every plate shows plate number, common name and italic accepted name; index links and native record disclosures work with a keyboard; the page states it is not an identification guide.
