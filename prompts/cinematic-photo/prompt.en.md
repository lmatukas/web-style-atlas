## Original creative brief

Create your own visual expression: original copy, illustrations, composition and details. Use only your own or properly licensed assets. Do not carry over other brands, code, recognisable scenes or combinations of them. Mark the working demonstration name clearly as invented; use real facts only once the user provides reliable content.

The project is DUSKLORE, an invented visual journal that pairs landscape photographs with a few unhurried lines of reflection. The direction depends on real photography: use the user's own photographs, properly licensed ones, or clearly labelled generated images, and record the author, source, licence and changes of each one. Never use a gradient or grey box as a stand-in for a photograph. Do not present unrelated photographs as one real journey, and do not name real places unless the user supplies verified captions.

Palette: near-black green #111817, a dark surface #1b2624, off-white text #f1f3eb, muted text #b2beb7, a pale brass accent #c3bc93 (also the focus outline) with dark text #171b16, and rules #79887e. Use Georgia (or a licensed editorial serif) for the few narrative headings and Arial for captions, labels and practical text. Text sits on the dark ground or a solid band, never as small pale type over a busy bright part of an image.

Use two photographs for the demonstration: a misty forest slope and a calm lake at sunset reflecting cool clouds. Resize them locally, strip metadata and keep each large enough to show composition and weather.

Composition A, the cover: a narrow masthead with the journal name, a tagline and small local links (field stories, our approach, the notebook). The large opening photograph — the forest in mist — framed by dark space, with a short title ("Where the world goes quiet.") near its lower edge on a reliable dark area, a link into the notebook, and a discreet credit caption beneath. A generous introduction explains the journal in a few deliberate lines and says the photographs come from different places. A selected scene pairs the lake photograph with a short observation and a link to it in the notebook. A short note on pace closes the page.

Composition B, the notebook: a centred title ("A pause between places."), one line and a compact chapter index (01 / Into the mist, 02 / The last light). Two numbered scenes follow in a narrow vertical filmstrip; each gives its photograph its own frame, a caption with the credit, a short heading and two short paragraphs. A final reflection closes the sequence, followed by a native disclosure with full photograph sources and licences and a return link.

Atmosphere comes from crop, negative space and order, never from scroll-jacking, forced parallax, autoplay video or a fake player. On a phone each photograph runs full width with its caption directly below, titles move off the image onto the dark ground when space is tight, and the notebook keeps one scene per screen-height or less.

## Final check

Check 360, 390, 768 and 1440 px widths for both compositions. There is no required motion: no animated image movement, parallax or autoplay; prefers-reduced-motion removes short link transitions. Check keyboard focus, contrast on every real text background, the console and that there is no horizontal scrolling. A static screenshot alone does not prove that a control works; operate each one.

Functional acceptance: Every photograph is rights-cleared, credited in a disclosure and described by alt text; chapter links reach the scenes; no text sits on busy bright areas; no autoplay, parallax, carousel, player or booking.

Mark the demonstration project clearly in the page footer.

Deliver a working preview, a summary of the checks actually run, the origin of assets and the remaining limits. Publish publicly only when the user asks. Finish the implementation; a plan alone is not a result.
